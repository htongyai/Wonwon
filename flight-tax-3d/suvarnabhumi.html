A model of Bangkok's main airport built from OpenStreetMap: its three runways, taxiways, aprons, the main terminal, SAT‑1, the control tower and the surrounding roads. Planes fly real routes from BKK. Since 20 June 2026, each passenger leaving on an international flight pays a ฿1,120 service charge, up from ฿730. Domestic flights still pay ฿130.

Who is on board

Click any plane above to open its cabin. Each figure is one passenger, colored by why they are flying. The flight strip shows what each group now pays.

The mix of travel reasons on each route is illustrative, not measured. It reflects known patterns: Thai workers mostly go to Taiwan, Korea, Japan, Israel and Malaysia; migrant workers fly home to Myanmar and Laos; students head to the UK. Fees are per passenger on a one-way departure.

Passenger numbers assume about 85% of seats are filled. The charge is included in the ticket price, so passengers don't pay it separately at the airport.

Traffic follows Bangkok time. The daily total is real: Suvarnabhumi handled 370,832 take-offs and landings in 2025, about 1,016 a day (AOT via Wikipedia). How that splits by hour is estimated, since live schedules can't be loaded here. Use the time slider to see another hour.

The flights are simulated. The routes and airlines are real services from Suvarnabhumi, but the timings, aircraft types and passenger numbers are approximate. The fees are the official AOT amounts. The airport layout, runways, taxiways, aprons, terminals and roads come from OpenStreetMap, the same data as the flood map. Road colours show the iTIC traffic snapshot from 28 Sep 2026. The terminal access viaducts and the Airport Rail Link (blue) are raised; their heights are approximate. Building heights are exaggerated 2.5×, and taxi routes are approximate.
